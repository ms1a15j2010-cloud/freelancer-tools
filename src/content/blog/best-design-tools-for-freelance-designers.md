The right design tool depends less on which one is "best" and more on what you design. A freelancer making social graphics for small businesses needs something different from one building app interfaces or illustrating on a tablet. Picking the wrong tool wastes hours, and picking an expensive one too early wastes money.

Here are six popular options, what each is good for, and the trade-offs. Free plans, limits and prices change often, so check each company's pricing page before you commit.

## Quick picks

- **Social posts, presentations and quick marketing graphics:** Canva
- **Website and app interface design:** Figma
- **Polished brand graphics, especially if you already use Adobe:** Adobe Express
- **Professional vector and layout work without a subscription:** Affinity
- **An open-source alternative to Figma:** Penpot
- **Illustration and sketching on an iPad:** Procreate

## Canva

Canva is the easiest place to start, with a huge template library and a free plan that covers most basic needs. Its paid Pro plan is listed at roughly $13 to $15 a month. The free plan limits some premium elements and features, and designs can look similar if you lean heavily on templates.

**Good for:** designers who make social media graphics, presentations and marketing materials quickly.

## Figma

Figma is the standard for interface and product design, with real-time collaboration that clients and teams like. It has a free plan with limits on the number of files, and paid plans for a single professional are listed at roughly $12 to $16 a month depending on the source and billing. It has a learning curve and is less suited to print design.

**Good for:** designers building websites, apps and design systems.

## Adobe Express

Adobe Express is a lighter, template-based design tool with a free plan and paid plans reported at roughly $8 to $10 a month. It includes Adobe's AI features and works well alongside Adobe's other apps. The free plan limits exports and advanced features, and the full Adobe Creative Cloud costs considerably more.

**Good for:** designers who want polished brand graphics, especially if clients already use Adobe.

## Affinity

Affinity offers professional vector, photo and layout tools without a subscription. Two 2026 sources describe it as free for individuals through a Canva account, but this kind of offer can change, so confirm it on Affinity's own site. It handles print work well, including layouts and PDF export for printers.

**Good for:** freelancers who want Adobe-level tools without paying a monthly fee.

## Penpot

Penpot is open source and works much like Figma, so it suits designers who prefer free and open tools. It has a smaller community and fewer plugins than Figma.

**Good for:** designers and developers who want a free, open-source interface design tool.

## Procreate

Procreate is a drawing and illustration app for the iPad, and it is typically sold as a one-time purchase rather than a subscription. It is excellent for illustration and sketching but is not made for layout, web design or client collaboration. It only runs on iPad.

**Good for:** illustrators and artists who work on a tablet.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **What do I mostly design?** Social graphics point to Canva, interfaces to Figma, illustrations to Procreate, and print work to Affinity or Adobe.
2. **What do my clients use?** If clients send Figma or Adobe files, you may need to match them.
3. **Can I start free?** Canva, Figma, Adobe Express and Penpot all have free plans, so try one before you pay.

If you are just starting, begin with Canva for quick work or Figma for interface work, and add a second tool only when a real project needs it.

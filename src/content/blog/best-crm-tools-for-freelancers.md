Most freelancers do not lose clients because the work is bad. They lose them because a lead went unanswered, a follow-up got forgotten, or a past client was never contacted again. A CRM, short for customer relationship management tool, keeps your leads, clients, notes and next steps in one place so nothing slips.

You do not need an enterprise system. Here are six options that suit one-person businesses, what each is good for, and the trade-offs. Plans and prices change often, so check each company's pricing page before you commit.

## Quick picks

- **Want a free tool to start with:** HubSpot CRM
- **Want a visual sales pipeline:** Pipedrive
- **Want a budget tool that can grow:** Zoho CRM
- **Live in Gmail all day:** Streak
- **Want proposals, contracts and invoices attached to each client:** Bonsai or HoneyBook

## HubSpot CRM

HubSpot's CRM has a free plan that covers contacts, a deal pipeline and email tracking. Sources disagree on whether there is a contact limit on the free plan, so check the current limits. Paid plans start at around $20 a month. The main drawback is that features locked behind higher plans can get expensive as you grow.

**Good for:** freelancers who want a capable free CRM to start with.

## Pipedrive

Pipedrive is built around a visual pipeline, where you move each lead from first contact to signed client. It has no permanent free plan, and plans start at around $14 per user per month. It is a better fit if you chase many leads than if you keep a small list of regular clients.

**Good for:** freelancers who pitch lots of prospects and want to see them at a glance.

## Zoho CRM

Zoho CRM has a free edition and low-priced paid plans, and it connects with other Zoho tools, including Zoho Invoice and Zoho Books. It is more customizable than simpler tools, which also means more setup time.

**Good for:** budget-conscious freelancers, especially those already using other Zoho products.

## Streak

Streak adds CRM features inside Gmail, so you can track leads without leaving your inbox. It has a free tier, and paid plans cost more per seat than some competitors, so check the price against the features you need. It only works with Gmail.

**Good for:** freelancers who do most of their client work by email in Gmail.

## Bonsai

Bonsai combines a client list with proposals, contracts, time tracking and invoicing. Plans start at around $25 a month. It is less of a sales tool than a full workflow tool, so it fits best if you want everything about each client in one place.

**Good for:** writers, developers and consultants who want one tool from proposal to payment.

## HoneyBook

HoneyBook combines a client pipeline with proposals, contracts, invoices and scheduling. It is popular with creative freelancers. It is only available to business owners in a limited set of countries, which included the U.S., Canada, the UK and Australia when we checked, so confirm that it works where you live before you spend time on it. It has no permanent free plan that we could verify.

**Good for:** creative freelancers in supported countries who want one place for the whole client process.

## When you do not need a CRM yet

If you have only a handful of clients, a simple spreadsheet or a project management board may be enough. A CRM starts to pay off when you have more leads or past clients than you can remember.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Do I mostly need to track leads, or run the whole client process?** For leads, pick HubSpot or Pipedrive. For the whole process, look at Bonsai or HoneyBook.
2. **Where do I do my client work?** If it is Gmail, try Streak.
3. **Is the tool available in my country?** Check this first, especially for HoneyBook.

If you are just starting, use a free plan and move to a paid one when you outgrow it.

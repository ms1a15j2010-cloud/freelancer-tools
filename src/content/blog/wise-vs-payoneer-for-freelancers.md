If you work with clients in other countries, getting paid is not as simple as sending an invoice. Wise and Payoneer are two of the most popular ways for freelancers to receive international payments, and they solve the same problem in different ways. Picking the wrong one can quietly cost you a few percent of every payment.

The short version: Wise is usually the better choice when clients pay you directly by bank transfer, because its pricing is transparent and its exchange rates are close to the real market rate. Payoneer is usually the better choice when you get paid through a freelance marketplace, because many platforms pay out to it directly. Fees, limits and country availability change often, so check each company's pricing page and confirm it works where you live before you commit.

## At a glance

| | Wise | Payoneer |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Direct bank transfers from clients | Marketplace payouts such as Upwork and Fiverr |
| How it charges | Transparent fees on top of the mid-market exchange rate | Fees by payment type, plus an exchange rate markup in some cases |
| Local receiving details | Yes, in several major currencies | Yes, in several currencies |
| Marketplace support | Limited | Strong |
| Availability | Varies by country | Wide, in many countries |

## How the fees differ

Wise is known for showing you what a transfer costs. It uses the real mid-market exchange rate and adds a visible fee, which sources put at somewhere between about 0.35% and 2.85% depending on the currency route. If money arrives through the international SWIFT network instead of local bank details, a separate receiving fee can apply, and banks along the way may take their own cut.

Payoneer charges depending on how you are paid. Receiving from another Payoneer user is free, and marketplaces often pay out with no extra fee on the Payoneer side. Receiving a direct bank transfer from a client can carry a fee of around 1%, and withdrawing to your bank can add a fee. Several sources also point to an exchange rate markup when Payoneer converts currency, which can raise the real cost. Because the totals depend so much on the route, compare the full cost from your client's payment to your bank account, not only the receiving fee.

In most comparisons for direct client payments, Wise comes out cheaper. For work paid through marketplaces, Payoneer can be the simpler option even if the all-in cost is higher.

## How you get paid

- **Wise:** you get local bank details in several currencies, so a client in the United States, the United Kingdom or Europe can pay you as if you had a local account. The money then sits in your Wise balance until you withdraw it.
- **Payoneer:** many marketplaces, including Upwork and Fiverr, can pay straight into a Payoneer account. It also offers receiving accounts in several currencies for direct client payments.

## Availability: check this first

Both services work in many countries, but not all of them in the same way. Payoneer describes support in 190+ countries and 70+ currencies. Wise's features, including which currencies you can receive and whether you can receive at all, depend on your country, and the rules have changed in some countries during 2026. Confirm that your country is supported and that you can receive payments before you give a client your details.

## Who should pick Wise

- Your clients pay by direct bank transfer.
- You want clear, upfront costs and a rate close to the real exchange rate.
- You work with clients in the United States, the United Kingdom, Europe or Australia, where local receiving details are most useful.

## Who should pick Payoneer

- You find work through a marketplace that pays out to Payoneer.
- Wise is not available or limited where you live.
- You want one account that works across several marketplaces and clients.

## Our recommendation

If you are paid directly by clients and Wise works in your country, start with Wise. If most of your income comes through marketplaces, or Wise is not an option for you, start with Payoneer. Some freelancers use both, with each client paying through whichever suits them. If you do this, track which account each client uses so your records stay clean.

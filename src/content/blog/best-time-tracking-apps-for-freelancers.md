If you bill by the hour, every untracked hour is unpaid work. Even if you charge by the project, tracking your time shows you which clients are actually worth it. A good time tracker is quick to start, works on your phone and computer, and turns your hours into invoices without retyping.

Here are five popular options, what each is good for, and the trade-offs. Free plans and prices change often, so check each company's pricing page before you commit.

## Quick picks

- **Want a free tool with plenty of features:** Clockify or Toggl Track
- **Want hours turned into invoices easily:** Harvest
- **Want a simple tool built for solo work:** My Hours
- **Want time logged for you automatically:** Timely

## Toggl Track

Toggl Track is known for a clean, fast timer that works on the web, desktop and mobile. Its free plan allows unlimited time entries, projects and clients for a small number of users, and paid plans start at around $9 per user per month. It connects with many other tools, including project management apps.

**Good for:** freelancers who want a polished timer and clear reports.

## Clockify

Clockify is popular because its free plan is generous, with unlimited time tracking. The free plan does not include invoicing, which sits on paid plans that start at roughly $4 to $6 per user per month. Sources disagree on the current user limit for the free plan, so check it before you sign up.

**Good for:** budget-conscious freelancers who mainly need reliable tracking.

## Harvest

Harvest is built around turning tracked time into invoices, and it also tracks expenses and receipts. The free plan is limited to one user and two projects, and paid plans cost roughly $11 to $12 per seat per month, higher than most competitors.

**Good for:** freelancers who want billing and time tracking to work together.

## My Hours

My Hours is a simple tracker designed for freelancers and small teams. It has a free plan for basic solo use and paid plans starting at roughly $8 per user per month. It covers billable hours, projects and client-ready reports without much setup.

**Good for:** solo freelancers who want something clean and uncomplicated.

## Timely

Timely tracks your work automatically in the background, so you do not have to remember to start a timer. It is useful if you jump between many tasks and forget to log them. It is a paid tool, so check the current price and free trial.

**Good for:** freelancers who forget to start timers.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Do I want to start timers myself or have it done for me?** If you forget, look at Timely.
2. **Do I want invoices in the same tool?** If yes, look at Harvest or a paid Clockify plan.
3. **Is a free plan enough?** For most new freelancers, Toggl Track, Clockify or My Hours is enough to start.

Try a free plan for a week or two before you pay. The best tracker is the one you actually remember to use.

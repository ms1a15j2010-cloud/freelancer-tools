When you work alone, you are also the project manager. Missed deadlines, forgotten requests and messy files cost you clients, and a good project management tool prevents most of that. The best one for you is the simplest one you will actually open every day.

Here are seven popular options, what each is good for, and the trade-offs. Plans and prices change often, so check each company's pricing page before you commit.

## Quick picks

- **Want one place for notes, tasks and client pages:** Notion
- **Want a simple visual board:** Trello
- **Want a free, lightweight to-do list:** Todoist
- **Want something that grows into a team tool:** Asana or ClickUp
- **Want client communication in one place:** Basecamp
- **Want project management plus invoicing and contracts:** Bonsai

## Notion

Notion combines notes, task lists, wikis and client pages in one flexible workspace. It has a generous free plan, and paid plans start at around $10 per user per month. The trade-off is setup time: you have to build your own system, and it is weak at invoicing, so pair it with a billing tool.

**Good for:** solo freelancers who want a single hub for everything.

## Trello

Trello organizes work as cards on boards, moving from "To do" to "Doing" to "Done." It is easy to learn in minutes, has a free plan, and paid plans start at around $5 per user per month. It is less suited to complex projects with many dependencies.

**Good for:** freelancers who think visually and want a quick way to see work in progress.

## Todoist

Todoist is a clean, fast task list with due dates, recurring tasks and projects. It has a free plan, and the paid plan costs around $4 a month. It does not manage client files or billing, so it works best as a companion to another tool.

**Good for:** freelancers who mostly need a reliable daily to-do list.

## Asana

Asana handles tasks, deadlines and timelines with a free plan for small use. Reporting and more advanced timeline features sit behind paid plans. It can feel like more than a solo freelancer needs, but it scales well if you start hiring help.

**Good for:** freelancers who expect to grow into a small team.

## ClickUp

ClickUp offers a large set of features, including tasks, docs, views and time tracking, in one app. That flexibility is a strength and a weakness, because it takes longer to set up and can feel crowded at first.

**Good for:** freelancers who like to customize and want many features in one tool.

## Basecamp

Basecamp keeps message boards, to-do lists and schedules together, which makes it easy to share progress with clients without long email threads. It has a simpler structure than most tools, with less room for customization.

**Good for:** freelancers who manage a lot of client communication.

## Bonsai

Bonsai combines project management with contracts, time tracking and invoicing. Plans start at around $25 a month, and it has changed its pricing model recently, so read the current plan details carefully.

**Good for:** freelancers who want one tool covering the whole client process, from proposal to payment.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Do I need to share work with clients?** If yes, look at Basecamp or Bonsai.
2. **Do I want to build my own system or use a ready-made one?** Notion and ClickUp reward tinkering, while Trello and Todoist work immediately.
3. **Do I plan to hire help later?** If so, Asana or ClickUp will grow with you.

If you are just starting, try a free plan first. Trello, Todoist and Notion all have one, so you can test before you spend anything.

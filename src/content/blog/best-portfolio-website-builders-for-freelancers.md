Clients check your work before they hire you. A simple portfolio website gives them one link to see your best projects, your services and how to contact you. You do not need to code or hire a designer, because several builders let you launch a professional site in a day.

Here are six popular options, what each is good for, and the trade-offs. Plans and prices change often, so check each company's pricing page before you commit.

## Quick picks

- **Want a free start:** Wix or Framer
- **Want polished templates with little effort:** Squarespace
- **Want full design control without writing code:** Webflow
- **Want a simple one-page site, very cheaply:** Carrd
- **Want an all-in-one portfolio plus client tools:** Pixpa

## Wix

Wix is a drag-and-drop builder with a free plan and hundreds of templates, including many for portfolios. The free plan shows Wix branding and does not allow a custom domain, so most freelancers upgrade. Paid plans start at roughly $17 a month when billed annually. One drawback often mentioned is that you cannot easily switch templates after you have built your site.

**Good for:** freelancers who want to start free and keep full control of the layout.

## Squarespace

Squarespace is known for polished, professional templates that look good with little effort. It offers a free trial rather than a free plan, and paid plans start at roughly $16 to $19 a month. A custom domain is included on annual plans, which saves a separate cost.

**Good for:** designers, photographers and creatives who want a finished look fast.

## Webflow

Webflow gives designers detailed control over layout and animation without writing code. It is free to design on, and site plans start at roughly $14 a month. It has a steeper learning curve than Wix or Squarespace, so expect to spend some time learning it.

**Good for:** designers and developers who want precise control and plan to grow the site.

## Framer

Framer builds sites quickly, with a free plan and low-priced paid plans starting at roughly $5 to $15 a month. It is popular for modern, animated portfolios, and its templates make it easy to get a site live quickly.

**Good for:** designers who want a striking portfolio live fast.

## Carrd

Carrd builds simple one-page sites at a very low price. It is a good fit if all you need is a page with a short introduction, a few examples and a contact link. It is not meant for large, multi-page portfolios.

**Good for:** freelancers who want a clean one-page site cheaply.

## Pixpa

Pixpa combines a portfolio site with extra tools such as galleries, a blog and client features. It offers a free trial and low starting prices of about $4 to $9 a month, depending on the source. It has fewer templates than Wix or Squarespace.

**Good for:** photographers and creatives who want a portfolio plus client tools at a low price.

## What every portfolio needs

Whichever builder you choose, include:

1. **A short introduction** saying who you help and what you do.
2. **Three to six of your best projects**, with a sentence on the result for each.
3. **A clear contact link or form.**
4. **A fast, mobile-friendly design**, since many clients will open it on a phone.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **How much design control do I want?** If a lot, look at Webflow or Framer. If little, Squarespace.
2. **Do I want to start free?** Wix and Framer have free plans, but you will likely need a paid plan for a custom domain.
3. **Do I need only one page?** If yes, Carrd may be all you need.

Pick one, build a simple first version this week, and improve it later. A live portfolio with a few strong projects wins more clients than a perfect one that never launches.

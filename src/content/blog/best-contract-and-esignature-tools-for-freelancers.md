Starting work without a signed agreement is one of the most common freelancer mistakes. A simple contract protects your payment, your deadlines and the scope of the job. An e-signature tool lets clients sign from their phone or laptop in a minute, with no printing or scanning.

Here are six popular options, what each is good for, and the trade-offs. Free plans and prices change often, so check each company's pricing page before you commit.

## Quick picks

- **Already have a contract and only need signatures:** PandaDoc, SignWell or Zoho Sign
- **Want it free and open source:** DocuSeal
- **Clients expect a well-known name:** DocuSign
- **Want proposals, contracts and invoices in one flow:** HoneyBook (or Bonsai, covered in our invoicing guide)

## PandaDoc

PandaDoc offers a free e-signature plan that lets you upload your own contract, add signature fields and send it. Paid plans start at around $19 per user per month and add templates, a content library and analytics. The free plan does not include proposals or templates.

**Good for:** freelancers who already have a contract and need only the signature step.

## SignWell

SignWell is often recommended for solo workers because of its free plan. It covers the basics of sending and signing documents without a big monthly bill. Check the current monthly limit on the free plan before you rely on it.

**Good for:** freelancers who send a few contracts a month and want to pay nothing.

## Zoho Sign

Zoho Sign has a free plan that allows a small number of documents each month for one user. It fits well if you already use other Zoho products such as Zoho Invoice, and its paid plans are fairly affordable.

**Good for:** freelancers already in the Zoho ecosystem.

## DocuSign

DocuSign is the name most clients recognize, which can matter when you work with larger companies. Paid plans start at roughly $10 to $11 a month for a small number of sends. Reports differ on whether it has a free plan, so check its pricing page.

**Good for:** freelancers whose clients expect DocuSign.

## DocuSeal

DocuSeal is open source, so you can host it yourself for free with no document limit. There is also a hosted version with a small free allowance each month. Self-hosting takes some technical work.

**Good for:** technical freelancers who want control and a low cost.

## HoneyBook

HoneyBook bundles proposals, contracts and invoices into one client flow. Plans cost more than a simple signature tool, at around $30 to $40 a month, but it replaces several tools at once.

**Good for:** creative freelancers who want one flow from proposal to payment.

## A note on contracts and the law

An e-signature tool only collects the signature. The contract itself still needs the right terms, such as scope, price, payment dates and deadlines. If the job is large, consider having a lawyer review your template once and then reuse it. The legal standing of electronic signatures also varies by country, so check the rules where you and your client live. This article is general information, not legal advice.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Do I already have a contract template?** If yes, a simple signature tool is enough.
2. **How many contracts do I send each month?** Free plans usually limit the number.
3. **Do my clients expect a specific tool?** If they ask for DocuSign, it may be easier to use it.

If you are just starting, try a free plan first and upgrade only when you outgrow the limit.

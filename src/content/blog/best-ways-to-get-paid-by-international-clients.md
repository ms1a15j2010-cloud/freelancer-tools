Winning a client in another country is the easy part. Getting paid is where fees, slow transfers and currency markups can quietly take a big share of your income. The right payment method can save you several percent on every invoice, and it can also decide how quickly the money arrives.

Here are the main options, what each is good for, and the trade-offs. Fees, limits and country availability change often and differ a lot from one country to another, so check each service's official pricing page and confirm it works where you live before you rely on it.

## Quick picks

- **Recurring clients paying in a foreign currency:** Wise
- **Marketplace work such as Upwork or Fiverr:** Payoneer
- **Clients who insist on a familiar name:** PayPal
- **Clients paying by card:** Stripe
- **Large one-off payments with no other option:** a bank wire

## Wise

Wise is built around transparent international transfers, and most comparisons find it cheaper than PayPal or a bank wire for currency conversion. You can usually get local bank details in several currencies, so a client can pay as if it were a local transfer. Availability and features, including which currencies you can receive, depend on your country, so check this first.

**Good for:** freelancers with repeat clients abroad who want to cut currency conversion costs.

## Payoneer

Payoneer is closely tied to freelance marketplaces, and many of them pay out straight to a Payoneer account. Some sources describe it as a practical option in countries where Wise coverage is limited. Fees depend on the type of transfer and withdrawal, so read the current pricing before you use it for direct client payments.

**Good for:** freelancers who earn through marketplaces, or live where other options are limited.

## PayPal

PayPal is the most widely recognized way to pay a freelancer, which makes it easy for clients. Comparisons usually find it the most expensive option for international payments, because of transaction fees plus a currency conversion markup. Whether you can receive and withdraw money also differs by country.

**Good for:** small payments from clients who will not use anything else.

## Stripe

Stripe lets clients pay by card, which many clients like because it is fast and familiar. It is popular for invoicing and recurring billing, and some freelancers move the balance to Wise or a bank account afterward. Stripe is only available in certain countries, so check whether you can open an account where you live.

**Good for:** freelancers who bill by card, especially recurring clients.

## Bank wire transfer

A direct bank wire needs no extra account, but it is often the slowest option. Fees can be charged by your bank, your client's bank and an intermediary bank, and the exchange rate is often poor. For smaller payments, those costs add up quickly.

**Good for:** large payments, or clients who can only pay by wire.

## Marketplace payouts

If you find work through a platform such as Upwork or Fiverr, the platform usually decides how and when you are paid and which withdrawal methods you can use. Platform commissions come on top of payment fees, so count both when you set your prices.

**Good for:** freelancers who prefer to let a platform handle collection and protect against non-payment.

## Protect yourself from payment scams

1. **Never accept an overpayment** and refund the difference. This is a common scam.
2. **Wait for the money to clear** before delivering final files.
3. **Use invoices and written agreements** so every payment has a record.
4. **Ask for a deposit** on larger jobs.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Which of these work in my country?** Check this first, because availability varies widely.
2. **What currency do my clients pay in?** If it differs from yours, compare the conversion cost.
3. **How often and how much will I be paid?** For small, rare payments, convenience may matter more than fees. For large or regular ones, a small fee difference becomes real money.

A common setup is to use one low-cost option for regular clients and keep a second as a backup for clients who refuse it.

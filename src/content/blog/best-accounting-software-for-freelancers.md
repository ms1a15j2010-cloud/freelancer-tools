Invoices tell clients what they owe you. Accounting software tells you what you actually earned. It records income and expenses, sorts your bank transactions, and gives you clean reports when tax time comes. Doing this in a spreadsheet works for a while, but the time you save with proper software is usually worth it.

Here are five popular options, what each is good for, and the trade-offs. Prices and plans change often, so check each company's pricing page before you commit.

## Quick picks

- **Want to pay nothing:** Wave or Zoho Books
- **Want the easiest tool for client billing:** FreshBooks
- **Want the industry standard that accountants know:** QuickBooks
- **Want clean, simple accounting that can grow:** Xero

## Wave

Wave is a free option for tracking income and expenses, with a paid plan that adds extra features. It suits freelancers with simple finances. Card payments from clients carry processing fees, so factor that in.

**Good for:** freelancers on a tight budget who need straightforward bookkeeping.

## FreshBooks

FreshBooks started as an invoicing tool and is easy to use. It combines invoices, expenses and light accounting, and plans start at roughly $23 a month. Several reviews note that cheaper plans limit the number of clients and that prices have risen over time.

**Good for:** freelancers who bill clients regularly and want a smooth experience.

## QuickBooks

QuickBooks is the name most accountants know, so it makes working with an accountant easier. Plans range from roughly $20 to $40 a month depending on which version you pick, and the plan structure can be confusing, so read the options carefully. Some plans include help estimating quarterly taxes in the US.

**Good for:** freelancers who work with an accountant or expect their finances to get more complex.

## Xero

Xero offers clean accounting with bank feeds and reports. Starting prices are reported anywhere from about $15 to $25 a month, so check the current figure. The cheapest plan leaves out some features, such as certain reports and bank reconciliation, so compare plans before you decide.

**Good for:** freelancers who want simple accounting with room to grow.

## Zoho Books

Zoho Books has a free plan for small businesses under a yearly revenue limit, which sources put at around $50,000, so check the current limit. Paid plans offer more depth and fit well if you already use other Zoho apps.

**Good for:** freelancers in the Zoho ecosystem or those who want more depth than Wave without a high price.

## A note on taxes

Many of these tools focus on tax rules from the United States, such as quarterly estimated payments and 1099 forms. If you live elsewhere, check which features work in your country and which currencies and tax rates the tool supports. This article is general information, not tax advice. A local accountant can tell you what you need to keep and file.

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Do I work with an accountant?** If yes, ask which tool they prefer.
2. **Do I mostly need invoices, or full bookkeeping?** If only invoices, you may not need accounting software yet.
3. **Does it support my country and currency?** Check this before you spend time setting it up.

If you are just starting, try a free plan first and upgrade when your finances get more complicated.
